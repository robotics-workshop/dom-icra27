Overview

Manipulating cloth, cables, food, and tissue is essential for robotics in manufacturing, logistics, healthcare, and domestic environments. Their evolving geometry, contact conditions, and material behavior make state partially observable and require continuous adaptation.

The topic is timely as foundation models and vision-language-action systems broaden robot capabilities, while tactile sensing, proprioception, and compliant actuation provide richer physical feedback. Connecting these advances raises three key questions:

  • Which states, including tension, slip, and contact, must be inferred beyond visual geometry?
  • How can physical models and learned representations support adaptation and recovery?
  • How should robustness across objects, tasks, and embodiments be evaluated?

These questions directly engage the sensing, control, and learning communities, with hardware and simulation providing supporting context. This event emphasizes the connection between multimodal state estimation and closed-loop action. Expected outcomes include shared research priorities, recommendations for evaluating robustness and recovery, and opportunities for collaborative experimental resources.

We welcome the comunity to this ICRA's workshop to follow the talks from our invited speakers offering perspectives from hardware, sensing, modeling, control, and learning, contributed spotlight talks, an interactive poster session, and a moderated discussion around shared challenges in deformable-object manipulation. Each invited talk will include dedicated Q&A, and speakers and contributors will be encouraged to present a failure case, limitation, or unresolved question alongside their results. Brief audience polls and an anonymous digital question channel will allow all attendees to shape the discussion, including those less comfortable speaking publicly.

Topics of Interest

  • Deformable and soft object manipulation
  • Embodied dexterity and contact-rich manipulation
  • Proprioceptive and tactile sensing
  • Compliant and dexterous robotics
  • Multimodal perception
  • Robot learning and vision-language-action models
  • Sim-to-real transfer

Invited Speakers


Manuel Catalano
Italian Institute of Technology

Talk Topics: Compliant Hands and Grasping

Short Bio: Manuel G. Catalano is a Principal Investigator at the Italian Institute of Technology, where he leads research on physical AI and human–robot co-evolution. His research focuses on soft robotics, compliant robotic hands, human–robot interaction, prosthetics, and rehabilitation. He contributed to the development of the Pisa/IIT SoftHand and other robotic systems designed for dexterous, adaptive physical interaction.


Kazuhiro Kosuge
City University of Hong Kong

Talk Topics: Textile Manipulation

Short Bio: Kazuhiro Kosuge is a Chair Professor in the Department of Mechanical Engineering at City University of Hong Kong. He is internationally recognized for his contributions to robotics, control engineering, human–robot interaction, and robot cooperation. He previously served as a professor and Distinguished Professor at Tohoku University and as President of the IEEE Robotics and Automation Society.


Tapomayukh Bhattacharjee
Cornell University

Talk Topics: Assistive Manipulation of Food and Cloth

Short Bio: Tapomayukh “Tapo” Bhattacharjee is an Assistant Professor in Cornell University's Department of Computer Science and directs the EmPRISE Lab. His research focuses on assistive robotics, human–robot interaction, haptic perception, and manipulation in unstructured environments. His work aims to help people with mobility limitations perform activities of daily living, including robot-assisted feeding and the manipulation of deformable objects.


David Navarro-Alarcon
The Hong Kong Polytechnic University

Talk Topics: Perception and Control for Deformable-Object Manipulation

Short Bio: David Navarro-Alarcon is an Associate Professor in the Department of Mechanical Engineering at The Hong Kong Polytechnic University, where he leads the Robotics and Machine Intelligence Laboratory (ROMI Lab). His research focuses on robot motion planning, learning, perception, and control, with particular emphasis on manipulation and the interaction between robots and deformable objects.


Uksang Yoo
UC Berkeley

Talk Topics: Mechanics-Informed Learning for Deformable Object Manipulation

Short Bio: Uksang Yoo is a postdoctoral researcher at UC Berkeley's AUTOLab, working with Professor Ken Goldberg. He received his Ph.D. from Carnegie Mellon University's Robotics Institute, where he was affiliated with the BIG and Momentum labs. His research explores how contact mechanics, tactile sensing, and physics-based models can improve robot learning and generalization in dexterous manipulation, including interactions with compliant and deformable objects.


Yunzhu Li
Columbia University
New York, USA

Talk Topics: World Models as Scalable Robotics Data Engines

Short Bio: Yunzhu Li is an Assistant Professor of Computer Science at Columbia University, where he directs the Robotic Perception, Interaction, and Learning Lab (RoboPIL). Before joining Columbia, he was an Assistant Professor at UIUC and a postdoc at the Stanford Vision and Learning Lab with Fei-Fei Li and Jiajun Wu; he received his PhD from MIT CSAIL, advised by Antonio Torralba and Russ Tedrake. His research in robot learning focuses on structured world models for deformable object manipulation, robotic foundation models, and multi-modal perception. His recognitions include the ICRA 2024 Best Conference Paper Award, the CoRL 2023 Best Systems Paper Award, and an Amazon Research Award.

Call For Papers

We invite short extended abstracts presenting research contributions and preliminary results on the topics of the workshop. We particularly encourage submissions from students, early-career researchers, underrepresented groups, and institutions or regions less frequently represented at major robotics conferences.

Submissions will undergo double-blind peer review based on relevance, technical quality, clarity, and potential to stimulate discussion. Up to 15 accepted contributions will be presented as posters in an interactive poster session, with a subset selected for spotlight presentations. Presenters are encouraged to address limitations, failure cases, or unresolved questions alongside successful results to highlight new research opportunities. Where authors grant permission, accepted abstracts and presentation materials will be made publicly available on this website.

Rules

  • All submissions must adhere to our double-blind review policy.
    • All author names and affiliations must not appear anywhere in the title or text of the submission or supplementary materials.
    • Acknowledgments to people or funding agencies must not appear in the submission.
    • There must be no links to external websites (e.g., YouTube, GitHub, institutional pages) in the submission.
    • All submission metadata must be anonymized.
  • All submissions must include the following, which do not count towards the page limit:
    • A Limitations section describing assumptions, failure modes, and other limitations with suggestions for future improvements.
    • An AI usage statement (even if no AI tools were used).
  • All authors must have an OpenReview profile, created at least two weeks before the submission deadline.
  • Papers submitted to the main conference cannot be submitted to this workshop.
  • Bot-generated submissions will be desk rejected.
  • All posters must be size A0 in portrait format.
  • Violation of any rules may result in desk rejection.
